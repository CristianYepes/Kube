## F1. What the agent already writes, and when [read 2026-10-10, `agent/src/kube_agent/episode.py`]

- `play()` writes every row of `episode.jsonl` and `decisions.jsonl` **as it happens** and flushes after each one
  (`write_row`, lines 101-103: "an episode that dies half way still leaves what it did").
- `episode.jsonl`: one row every 0.5 s of game time (`ROW_INTERVAL`), `{t, x, y, hp, wave, enemies, tactic}`, and a last
  row where the episode ended.
- `decisions.jsonl`, a row when a decision is made: for `jev`, when its answer is read, with `t` (the state's time),
  `answered_at`, `tactic`, `applied`, `reason` (`stale`, `episode_ended`, `below_threshold` or none), `confidence`,
  `answers.tactic.probabilities` (the four options), `state` (the words Jev read), `latency_ms`, `cost` and `model`.
  For `scripted`: `{t, state, tactic, applied}` only (23 rows in a T9 episode).
- `result.json` is written when the episode ends (`outcome`, `survival_seconds`, `damage_taken`, ...).
- **Consequence:** a separate process that reads the run folder while it grows sees each decision at most one polling
  interval after it is written, and it cannot slow the agent's loop down, because it does not run in it.
- The rules of the baseline are one function, `policies.scripted_tactic(state)`, over the same `state` Jev reads (four
  rules, lines 54-63). Applied to Jev's states, it gives "what the rules would have chosen" with no second episode.
  R17: they agree on 91.0% of Jev's decisions on `subject_map` and 97.7% on `pillars`.

## F4. Placing the game window

- The README of `xdotool` warns that under Wayland "this software will not work correctly", and window functions are
  among those listed; parts of it do work under Xwayland, with no promise about moving windows [read 2026-10-10 through a
  search summary of github.com/jordansissel/xdotool; no test of `windowmove` on GNOME under Xwayland was found].
  **Under an X11 session `xdotool windowmove` is the tool's normal use.**
- Ubuntu 22.04 offers "Ubuntu on Xorg" from the gear on the login screen on most machines; `WaylandEnable=false` in
  `/etc/gdm3/custom.conf` forces X11 for every login. The sources disagree about whether the gear is still there in 22.04
  [read 2026-10-10: linuxconfig.org, Launchpad bug 1968929, a Discourse thread]. **[not verified]** on the owner's
  machine: task V1.
- The game makes a new window at every episode, and the episode starts at once (the idle player dies in about 7 s), so
  the window cannot be dragged into place by hand before the game starts.

## Options

**How the decision is shown**

| Option | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A. A local web page fed by a small server that reads the run folder | **chosen** | full control of type and layout; stdlib only; a separate process that reads files cannot slow the game loop (F1); the same page can replay a recorded run on the Mac for design and tests |
| B. A terminal panel (for example with `rich`) | rejected | it is still "logs": the owner said that this look is ugly; monospace only; a new dependency |
| C. Text drawn inside the game window | rejected | changes the C code of the game or of its bridge (the owner must be asked, and `check_bridge.py` re-run); the game's text drawing is a bitmap font (`font.c`) |
| D. A Tk window | rejected | needs the `python3-tk` package and gives poor typography |
| E. A video composed by a program (game captured with `ffmpeg` plus a rendered panel) | rejected by the owner | the owner chose to record live (2026-10-10) |

**How the game window gets its place**

| Option | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| X11 session, then `xdotool search --sync --name '^cub3D$' windowmove` when a run starts, and `xwininfo` to read where it landed | **chosen, to be verified** (task V1, V7) | the normal use of the tool on X11 |
| Wayland with `center-new-windows`, the page laid out around wherever the window is | fallback | not verified; a centred window leaves only 320 px at the side on a 1920-wide screen |
| Drag by hand | rejected | a new window per episode, which starts playing at once (F4) |

| Option | Verdict | Why |

## Gotchas found while reading

- The game window gets a title bar from the window manager and GNOME has a top bar: the recorded portion starts at the
  top-left corner of the game's drawing area, not of its window, so neither is in the video.
- The window moves into place just after it appears (a fraction of a second, not measured): the first instant of a take
  is cut afterwards (QuickTime's Trim).
- The terminal that launches `kube-agent run` must not sit above the page: the launch waits a few seconds so that the
  owner can bring the page to the front first.
- `episode.jsonl` has a row every 0.5 s, so the time bar of the view moves in steps unless the page interpolates between
  rows; decisions arrive at their own time.
- A demo take is one game: it cannot show the result of the evaluation. The final card says where its numbers come from
  (R17: 120 games on held-out seeds).
